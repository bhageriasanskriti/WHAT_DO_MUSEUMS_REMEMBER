# Chapter Copy

This is the evidence-safe editorial copy for the first implementation. It intentionally avoids unsupported claims.

## 01 — What Do Museums Remember?

**Hero:**  
What Do Museums Remember?

**Supporting line:**  
An exploration of the objects, people, places and stories preserved in museum collections.

**Transition:**  
Every object has a record.  
A collection turns records into patterns.

## 02 — One Object Becomes a Collection

**Copy:**  
One object is a point.  
A collection is a field.

Watch the field grow.

**Important implementation note:** show each institution's own total before any cross-institution aggregate. The 504,349-record total is a combined research corpus, not one museum collection.

## 03 — What Do Museums Collect?

**Copy:**  
Objects begin to gather around what they have in common.

Prints. Photographs. Drawings. Textiles. Ceramics. Architecture.

The exact labels come from each museum's own classification system.

## 04 — When Does the Museum Remember?

**Copy:**  
Move through the dates recorded in the collection.

Some records give a year.  
Some give a range.  
Some give an approximate date.

**Question:**  
Is history preserved evenly?

Keep this as a question, not a conclusion.

## 05 — Who Gets Remembered?

**Copy:**  
Every record can carry an attribution.

Not every attribution is the same.

Some name a person.  
Some identify an unknown or unidentified maker.  
Some describe an attribution or role.

**Question:**  
What changes when a name enters the record?

## 06 — Where Does Culture Come From?

**Copy:**  
A place can describe an object.  
A nationality can describe a maker.  
A birthplace can describe a person.

They are not the same thing.

**Question:**  
What can a collection's geography actually tell us?

## 07 — Indian Makers, One Collection

**Copy:**  
The supplied MoMA data contains 229 records whose creator nationality is recorded as Indian.

This is a story about makers represented in one collection—not a map of where the objects came from.

## 08 — One Lens, Many Lives

**Copy:**  
59,031 photographic negatives.

One photographer.

Thousands of captions, faces, streets, homes, churches, clubs and gatherings recorded in the archive's titles.

**Question:**  
What becomes visible when a collection is read as a community archive?

The keyword counts are descriptive evidence, not counts of unique people or events.
